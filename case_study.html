Case study 01 · Fintech / KYC

AI-Enhanced KYC Onboarding

A six-step digital onboarding flow for an Indian investing app, designed so a first-time investor can fund an account the same day — and so no clean application ever needs a human to look at it.

Role
Product — problem framing, BRD & PRD, flow design, prototype
Surface
PocketPortfolio (consumer app) × Binvestt (KYC provider)
Artifacts
PRD, decision-rule spec, clickable prototype
Year
2026
Open the prototype ↗ Full PRD on Notion ↗

Step 1 of 6 — consent first, then identity. The Binvestt AI Decision Engine panel on the right logs every automated action as the applicant moves.

The problem

Digital KYC in India is where investing apps lose the customer they just paid to acquire. The applicant is asked for Aadhaar or PAN, a selfie, and a set of consents, and then waits — sometimes hours, sometimes a day — while a reviewer works a queue. Two things go wrong at once: good applicants drop off during the wait, and reviewers spend most of their day on applications that never needed a human at all.

The brief was not "add AI to KYC". It was: get a genuine applicant funded the same day, without loosening a single control the regulator cares about. That framing decides everything downstream — the AI is there to clear the easy cases so reviewers can be slow and careful on the hard ones, not to replace the reviewer.

Decisions

Consent is a gate, not a checkbox at the end

Problem
Consent language is usually buried at submission, so the applicant has already handed over an Aadhaar number before they've been told what happens to it. That's a weak consent record and a bad first impression.
Decision
Consent moves to step 1 and the Continue button stays locked until it's ticked. The copy names the partner, the purpose (KYC under the PMLA), where the data sits (servers in India, per RBI data-localisation norms), how long it's kept (5 years), and that it's never sold and can be withdrawn.
Outcome
The consent event is timestamped before any identity data is collected, so the audit trail is defensible by construction rather than reconstructed later.

The decision is risk-tiered, not binary

Problem
A single approve/reject threshold forces a bad trade: set it loose and fraud gets through, set it tight and you false-reject real customers — who almost never come back.
Decision
Three outcomes instead of two. High-confidence matches on a clean document auto-approve and fund the same day. Mid-confidence — a blurred capture, a name-order mismatch between PAN and Aadhaar, an address that won't parse — routes to a reviewer with the specific field flagged. Only hard signals (document tampering, a liveness fail, a watchlist hit) reject outright.
Outcome
False rejects become review items instead of lost customers, and the reviewer opens a case that already tells them which field to look at.

Liveness is matched against the document, not just detected

Problem
A liveness check on its own only proves a real person is holding the phone. It doesn't prove they're the person on the PAN card.
Decision
The selfie is scored twice — once for liveness (is this a live human, not a photo of a photo or a replay) and once for face match against the extracted document photo. Both have to clear their own threshold; a strong liveness score can't carry a weak match.
Outcome
Closes the most common cheap attack — a genuine-looking selfie paired with someone else's borrowed document.

Every automated action is written to a log the applicant can see

Problem
When an automated system rejects you and says nothing, you assume it's broken. When it rejects a compliance team and says nothing, they stop trusting it and re-review everything by hand — which erases the whole benefit.
Decision
The Decision Engine panel runs beside the form and logs each action as it happens: document parsed, liveness scored, risk tier assigned, routed to review. Same log, two audiences — reassurance for the applicant, an audit record for compliance.
Outcome
The system is legible while it runs, so a reviewer inherits context instead of starting from a blank case.

Retries are bounded and told the applicant why

Problem
Unlimited silent retries are both a fraud surface and the single most common reason genuine users abandon: they get told "try again" four times with no idea what's wrong.
Decision
A capped number of retries per step, each returning the actual reason — glare on the document, face not centred, expired ID. Past the cap the application moves to manual review rather than dead-ending.
Outcome
Nobody gets stuck in a loop, and the failure reason becomes an input to the reviewer instead of noise.

Metrics I'd hold it to

  • Onboarding completion rate — the headline. Everything else is a means to this.
  • Straight-through-processing rate — share of applications approved with no human touch. The number that says whether the AI is earning its cost.
  • False-reject rate — genuine applicants turned away. Watched harder than fraud caught, because a false reject is a customer who never returns.
  • Time to verified — consent to funded account, measured at p50 and p90. The p90 is the one that matters; averages hide the queue.
  • Cost per verification — document and liveness API spend plus reviewer minutes, per approved customer.
  • Review queue depth and ageing — an early warning that a threshold has drifted before it shows up in completion rate.

What's in the build

6-step onboarding flow Consent gate (PMLA) Aadhaar & PAN capture Document OCR & field extraction Live selfie / face liveness Face-to-document match Risk tiering Auto-approve · review · reject Human-in-the-loop queue Live decision audit log Bounded retries with reasons RBI data localisation 5-year retention & withdrawal

What I'd do next

  • Instrument the drop-off per step before touching anything else — the prototype assumes where people leave; real funnel data would probably move the priority.
  • Shadow-run the thresholds against a reviewed backlog, so auto-approve only goes live once the false-reject rate is known rather than estimated.
  • Give reviewers a feedback loop — every overturned decision becomes a labelled example, so the tiering gets tighter instead of drifting.
  • Re-KYC and remediation, which is the same engine pointed at existing customers and the larger operational cost over time.
